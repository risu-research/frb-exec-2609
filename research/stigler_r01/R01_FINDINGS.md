## Verified observations

- Hawaii SB3001 had **seven** official text versions (introduced, SD1, SD2, HD1, HD2, HD3, CD1), and five committee/conference reports. All 12 sources were captured with SHA-256 in the [reproducible R01 run](https://github.com/risu-research/frb-exec-2609/actions/runs/45284081365). See `capture_hawaii.py`.
- February 10 government hearing packet includes **Google's own proposed redline amendments** and **Aidgentic LLC's contrary small-integrator testimony**. Google sought 3h rather than hourly notices, deletion of the explicit guest branch, removal of affirmative inclusivity for embedded bots, and other changes: [official testimony PDF pp.9–11](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/Testimony/SB3001_TESTIMONY_CPN-LBT_02-10-26_.PDF).
- Senate committee chair, per the [third-party Digital Democracy hearing transcript](https://civilbeat.digitaldemocracy.org/hearings/278670), said the committee would adopt Google's proposals insofar as compatible with consumer protection agency changes. The [official SD1 committee report](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/CommReports/SB3001_SD1_SSCR2388_.HTM) and official SD1 text document multiple textual matches.
- **Intermediate uptake != final survival**: 3h disclosure was adopted in SD1, but the [conference report](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/CommReports/SB3001_CD1_CCR95-26_.HTM) restores hourly notices **for users known or reasonably certain to be minors**. Explicit guest branch deleted in SD1 and remains absent in CD1. Affirmative embedded-bot inclusion removed at SD1, distinct from the separate embedded-app *exemption*, which survived through HD1 and was removed at HD2. Bill's original broad scope became a 3-feature companion scope only at CD1.
- A **March 4 Google redline** sought a broader third-party model-developer shield and a narrower definition of targeted advertising. Neither textual formulation appeared in SD2 or final: [official March 4 hearing PDF pp.8–9](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2026/Testimony/SB3001_SD1_TESTIMONY_JDC_03-04-26_.PDF). The original, narrower model-developer shield did survive.
- AG and DCCA/OCP successfully proposed independent changes, giving strong alternative explanatory channels. The final `develops AND makes available` operator rule, not in Google's February proposed amendments, must remain **unattributed** pending direct source evidence.
- First-pass scenario matrices: 35 product-scope cells, 30 actor-role cells, 5 guest-duty cells; **not** expert-adjudicated liability findings.

## Novelty warning

The issue of Google influencing state chatbot bills was already deeply reported by [NPR, Sept 18 2026](https://www.knkx.org/2026-09-18/how-google-is-drafting-ai-chatbot-laws-around-the-country). Comparative law-definition work was already published by [FPF 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/the-chatbot-moment-mapping-the-emerging-2026-u-s-chatbot-legislative-landscape/). Exact testimony-to-amendment methods were used in [Kirsten Matoy Carlson, *Statutes and Special Interests*, 2025](https://scholarlycommons.law.northwestern.edu/nulr/vol119/iss5/4/). One Hawaii case cannot establish novelty or Stigler selection strength.

## Falsifiable next gate

Only investigate further if 2+ independent additional jurisdictions supply competing actor-specific written amendment proposals linked by time to all bill versions, **and** a defensible difference in resulting developer/platform/integrator responsibility or observable compliance burden. Track first-round uptake and final-survival rates separately, count explicitly rejected amendments, compare consumer safety agency requests, and never infer a causal lobbying effect from matching phrases alone. Otherwise stop.

Do not copy the hearing packets into the public repository: some pages contain individual contact information. Cite original URLs instead.
